Research

How well a person and several robots are teaming, estimated in real time, so the team can change who does what.

Current

One person, several robots

I am a PhD candidate in Robotics and AI in the Human-Machine Teaming Laboratory, advised by Julie Adams. The work estimates team proficiency during a mission: how the person is doing, what each robot can still do, and whether communication is holding. Those estimates are for changing who does what, and how the team interacts, before the mission fails. Wildland fire is the domain.

The person’s side comes from studies of one pilot supervising many uncrewed aircraft beyond visual line of sight. I design and run those studies, including wildland fire, with Christopher Sanchez. I built the real-time physiological signal processing, the machine-learning models, and the IMPRINT Pro workload models, and I program the autonomous robots in the work. The wildfire study is first-author, in review at Human Factors, with Alejandra Hilbert, Andrew Dassonville, Christopher Sanchez, and Julie Adams. Oregon State Engineering posted the wildland-fire study.

The published model is Workload Modeling for 1:N UAS Delivery Operations, HFES 2025, first author. In IMPRINT Pro, predicted pilot workload moved from 77.8 to 91.8 as the delivery fleet grew from the low condition to the high one. Every condition was already in overload, above 60. Unexpected events, especially two detect-and-avoid encounters, raised workload more than adding aircraft did. Cognitive and visual demand drove the change. Motor and speech demand stayed small. Sponsors of the adaptive teaming work include DARPA/NASA and AFOSR.

Earlier

Georgia Tech, STAR Lab, with Harish Ravichandar. 2021–2023.

  1. 2023

    Trait preferences

    First author. Expert demonstrations show which traits matter because the expert assigns the important ones consistently. Using those preferences made allocation more effective than treating every trait as equal, and let the optimizer drop traits. Sponsored by ARL.

    AAMAS extended abstract

  2. 2023

    Unknown task rewards

    Coauthor. I generated StarCraft II demonstrations to test the coalition-formation algorithm, which uses concurrent multi-task bandits when task rewards are unknown and the robots are limited.

    RSS 2023

Also at Georgia Tech, without a paper: trainability feedback inside decentralized allocation, and a personalized embedding for pose prediction.
